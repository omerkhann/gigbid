# Price‑elasticity study for a grocery delivery app

## Context
Bilal Ahmed was engaged by a retail‑focused client that operates a grocery delivery service. The business wanted to understand how sensitive their customers were to price changes across a range of product categories. Their existing reporting tools gave only high‑level revenue snapshots, but they lacked a systematic way to test the impact of different price points on both sales volume and overall basket profitability. The goal was to identify a handful of price adjustments that could improve the margin on each order without sacrificing customer satisfaction.

## What I built
I began by gathering several months of transaction data, cleaning it, and constructing a unified view of each shopper’s basket. Using statistical techniques, I measured how quantity purchased varied with price for each product group. To evaluate potential price moves, I set up controlled experiments where two groups of customers were shown different price levels for the same items, while all other factors remained identical. This allowed me to isolate the effect of price alone on buying behavior. I then built a model that translated the observed changes in demand into projected impacts on the overall margin of a typical basket. The model was wrapped in an interactive visual dashboard that let the client explore different scenarios, see the trade‑off between volume and profit, and quickly pinpoint the most promising adjustments.

## Results
The analysis identified four specific price changes that would lift the average margin per basket by 2.1 %. The client implemented these adjustments and reported an immediate uptick in profitability while maintaining stable order volumes. The interactive dashboard continues to be used by the client’s product team to test future pricing ideas, turning what was once a guess‑based process into a data‑driven decision engine.
